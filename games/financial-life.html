Every roll brings a circumstance; every decision shapes your response.

How to play Roll when prompted, choose a path, and follow your simulated life from age 18 to 65. The game applies each year’s amounts automatically, including age 65. Record your final score and three die results. Play again after reading the book using “Replay same rolls,” with the same marriage choice and number of children. Your journey

A simplified book game: the annual career budget has taxes and unlisted living costs already allowed for. Listed costs are deducted separately.

Rules & timing

Start at $0. At each age, make the choice and deduct any upfront cost, then apply annual amounts once. A negative score is a shortfall; keep playing. Cash car purchases and home down payments require a score that covers their full upfront amount. No interest is added to shortfalls.

College funding outcomes 1, 2, 3 and 6 cover costs with no leftover savings. Trades or direct work replaces the college funding outcome. Outcome 4 costs $10,000 or $20,000 annually at ages 18–21; outcome 5 costs $5,000 annually. College has no score change at 22. Trades save $10,000 annually at 18–22; work saves $5,000.

At 23, roll for an annual budget of $44,000, $34,000, $29,000, $24,000, $19,000 or $14,000. Add $5,000 for college or $2,500 for trades to that annual amount. Apply it at ages 23–65. These are fictional amounts after taxes and unlisted living costs, before housing and other listed costs. Subtract $24,000 for housing annually at ages 23–36; use your selected housing costs from 37. Training additions illustrate a possible later benefit, not actual career returns.

Transport runs at ages 24–65. Each car has a $4,000 annual running/replacement allowance plus its purchase cost: used $10,000 upfront; new $40,000 upfront; or financed $8,000 annually at 24–30. Transit is $1,500 annually. Relationship rolls 1–6 deduct $500, $2,000, $3,000, $4,000, $10,000 or $20,000 per year respectively at ages 27–29. If you marry, continue through age 65 and deduct a $10,000 or $40,000 wedding cost once at age 30. Otherwise, stop relationship spending before the age-30 amounts and skip the wedding.

Each child is born at your age 32. Living costs are $8,000 annually at 32–49. An RESP costs $2,500 per child annually at 33–46, then $1,000 at 47, and stops. The $36,000 contribution total plus $7,200 basic CESG covers the $43,200 education budget at your age 50, with no extra deduction then. Without an RESP, deduct $43,200 per child once at 50 ($36,000 + $7,200). This represents paying for education yourself, not a government penalty. Both paths use the same education budget. The game assumes full basic 20% CESG eligibility and no prior contributions, additional benefits or investment returns. Lifetime contributions across all RESPs cannot exceed $50,000 per child; government grants and bonds are excluded from that cap.

At 37, the fictional home price is $450,000. Pay 5% ($22,500), 10% ($45,000), 15% ($67,500) or 20% ($90,000) upfront if affordable. Annual mortgage payments at 37–61 are $30,000, $28,000, $26,000 or $23,000 respectively. Add $450,000 home value once at 62. Rent costs $24,000 annually at 37–65. The score includes no separate mortgage balance or extra RESP asset value.

For a learning comparison, use the same dice, marriage choice and child count. Compare money decisions within those family goals. See the CRA contribution rules and CESG explanation for real-world rules.
