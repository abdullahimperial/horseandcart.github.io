From transactions to a plan

Your financial reset.

See your direction. Find what deserves a closer look. Choose your next steps using the book’s saving pillars and emergency-system framework.

Your files stay with you.

This app reads CSVs in browser memory. No files or financial data are uploaded, stored in browser storage, or sent to an AI service. Reloading clears the session. Download your report if you want to keep it.

A statement is a starting point. Suggested categories and review flags need your confirmation. This app cannot prove fraud, verify benefits, or decide which investments are suitable.

02 · Correct the assumptions

Review before you conclude.

Confirm income, refunds, and transfers. A card payment is not a second purchase.

Choose the analysis window

Transaction dates alone cannot prove statement coverage. You choose and confirm the period. Known currency columns containing non-CAD or missing values block analysis.

Review transactions and suggested categories

Chequing credits categorized as “Income” or “Government benefits & support” count as income. Other chequing credits stay unresolved until you classify them as income, a refund category, or a transfer. Non-payment card credits are treated as purchase refunds; change unrelated credits to “Transfer / card payment” or “Exclude.” Mortgage and loan payments remain cash obligations, even when they include principal.

Your context matters

Emergency savings and stable surplus

Leave unknown amounts blank. Transactions do not establish your balances, essential budget, or future income. The app will keep these conclusions open rather than invent figures.

Debt is a separate check.

A loan payment or interest charge does not reveal the balance or APR. Review statements and agreements. “Expensive debt” here is a screening rule you choose; the app does not label every loan as bad debt.

Unknown rates leave the debt assessment incomplete. Compare penalties, minimum payments, household needs, and qualified advice before choosing repayment or investing.

Explore a possible improvement—not a promise

Enter changes you have identified and can sustain. Avoid double-counting savings. Proposed cuts are not assumed to be possible just because a category exists.

Inputs stay in session memory. Changing a transaction or context marks an existing report as out of date until you rebuild it.
