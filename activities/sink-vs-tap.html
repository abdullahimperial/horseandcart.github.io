Chapter 5 activity

Sink vs. Tap

Choose an item or purchase and examine its outcomes. In the book, an outcome that takes money from you is a sink; an outcome that brings money to your pocket is a tap.

Analyze something in your life



↓ Sinks

Outcomes that take money from you.

↑ Taps

Outcomes that bring money to your pocket.

Add as many relevant outcomes as you can before reviewing the item.

Try the three cases from the book

1. A college degree in the ancient Chinese craft of making cloth.
2. A luxury car with high mileage cost, high financing and maintenance costs, but which makes a great impression on friends and family.
3. A mortgaged house costing less than 30% of take-home monthly salary, versus a rental house with the same costs.

The purpose is to identify the money-taking and money-producing outcomes and think through the decision, rather than rely on the label alone.

A broader reminder from the book

The Sink vs. Tap discussion explicitly notes that some spending creates a greater good in society even when it may not be financially productive. The exercise is intended as a financial-decision lens, not a measure of everything that gives something value.
