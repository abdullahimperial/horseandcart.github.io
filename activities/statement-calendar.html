Important assumptions and limits

  • This is an educational illustration, not financial, credit, legal or tax advice, and not a recommendation to borrow or spend.
  • The 21-day period is an assumption for this activity. Federally regulated financial institutions must provide at least 21 days for purchases, but the dates and rules on your own statement and cardholder agreement control.
  • The activity assumes the selected purchase posts on the selected date, belongs to the statement period shown, and qualifies for a purchase grace period. A transaction that posts after the closing date may appear in the next statement period.
  • A purchase grace period generally depends on paying the required full statement balance by the due date. Cash advances, balance transfers and cash-like transactions may not have a grace period.
  • This is not a deferred-interest financing offer. It illustrates how purchase interest may be applied from the purchase date when the normal grace period is lost.
  • If the required balance is not paid in full, interest may be calculated from the purchase date. The estimate uses purchase amount × APR ÷ 365 × days, with no payments before the example payoff date. It is a simple approximation, not your issuer’s calculation.
  • Actual interest can differ because issuers use the account’s terms and balance calculation method, and because of other transactions, payment timing, daily balances, compounding, fees, rate changes, holidays and processing cutoffs. The activity does not estimate late fees, minimum payments, credit-score effects or interest on other balances.
  • Your statement, agreement and issuer are the authoritative sources for your closing date, due date, rates, grace-period eligibility, payment allocation and charges. Contact your issuer if you need account-specific information.
