What this truly is: a browser-only worksheet combining dated, sourced low-cost options with clearly marked illustrative allowances. It does not search prices, find the cheapest offers, calculate eligibility or refresh market data. Reloading resets the example; it does not update the figures. Offers and program details were checked on 1 November 2026. This is a limited source review, not proof of the cheapest available option. Other allowances are not researched market prices.

Household assumptions

Edmonton, Alberta example; two adults and one child aged 6–12, accompanied on regular ETS trips; rented housing; transit and walking; no car, debt or paid childcare. These are choices for this exercise, not a claim that this budget is sufficient or available for every household.

A low-income household may qualify for support, but eligibility for every program cannot be assumed. Housing subsidies may have waitlists, and some supports overlap or cannot be combined.

Monthly income

Benefits start at $0 because they have not been calculated. Replace this with a verified amount. For a quarterly payment, divide by 3; for an annual amount, divide by 12. A monthly average does not mean the money arrives monthly.

Do not count a discounted service as cash income as well as reducing its expense.

How these estimates are calculated
  • Annual amounts generally use 12 months of the stated reduction. The Koodo comparison uses ($50 × 365 ÷ 30) − $318 = about $290.33 before tax: 30-day billing differs from calendar-month billing. Monthly averages are rounded, so multiplying a displayed average by 12 can differ by a few cents. These are potential estimates, not a claim that every saving occurred together.
  • Subtract repair, switching or other extra costs when known. The phone comparison is before tax; tax treatment varies across the other source examples. Annual phone plans need upfront payment; covered service is not liquid emergency cash.
  • Transit uses the author’s stated $100-versus-$35 comparison, not a universal current fare. Ride Transit and Leisure Access are different programs with a shared application.
  • The author clarified his previous phone price as $25/month. His book describes an annual Koodo plan with unlimited Canada-wide calls and texts and 32 GB for the entire year. His exact historical annual payment and tax basis have not been confirmed. Koodo now advertises $159/365 days with 40 GB for the year and unlimited Canada-wide calling, new activations only. The table uses the current-price illustration, rather than treating today’s offer as his historical plan. See the plan details and billing-period calculator.
  • Netflix is a current-rate estimate for one household subscription, assuming free replacements. The autopay discount’s monthly frequency must be confirmed. Bottle returns recover deposits rather than create investment income.
  • If your budget already includes a cheaper bill, do not subtract the saving again. Count each reduced expense once.

Separate one-time benefits: $900. Bank joining bonuses of $350 + $450, plus a $100 water-heater discount. These are not recurring annual savings and are not included above.

Edmonton · Leisure Access

Recreation and family outings can cost less, too

Approved members of Edmonton’s Annual Leisure Access Program receive free admission to participating City recreation centres, pools and attractions. The paid Monthly Pass option has different terms. Each person attending needs the appropriate eligibility and admission coverage.

An example of the yearly effect: one otherwise-paid $36.50 recreation-centre visit each month would avoid $438 a year in drop-in fees. This is an illustration, not the author’s recorded visit frequency. Compare with any cheaper membership or multi-visit pass you would otherwise buy.

Fort Edmonton Park also accepts Leisure Access for regular summer admission; summer prices and attractions differ. Private and separately ticketed events are excluded. For the zoo, check whether a special event falls within regular admission. Check current hours and booking requirements before visiting.

Other participating options include the Muttart Conservatory and eligible outdoor pools. The Annual Program also offers 75% off eligible registered programs, within its annual limits: three programs per adult/senior and four per child. Personal training and private swimming lessons are excluded.

Count spending you would actually avoid. Family examples here use two adults and three children, unlike the one-child budget scenario above. Admission benefits have not been added to the annual total because visit frequency is unknown. Do not count both membership savings and drop-in fees for the same visits; travel, food and other outing costs remain separate.
